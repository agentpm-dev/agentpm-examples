# Support Response Principles

Use these principles when drafting or revising customer-facing responses.

## 1. Acknowledge the issue plainly

- Start by showing that you understand the problem in concrete terms.
- Name the symptom or workflow that is failing instead of responding with a generic apology.
- Avoid overstating certainty if the root cause is still under investigation.

## 2. Separate what is known from what is still being checked

- State confirmed facts first.
- Then identify what is still under review, what team owns it, and what the next checkpoint is.
- Do not imply an ETA unless one has been confirmed internally.

## 3. Give the customer a next-step anchor

- Every response should answer at least one of these questions:
  - What should the customer do now?
  - What will we do next?
  - When should they expect another update?
- If there is no action for the customer, say that directly.

## 4. Keep tone calm and specific

- Prefer short, concrete sentences over overly polished reassurance.
- Avoid phrases that sound dismissive, such as "just", "simply", or "should be fine now".
- If the customer is blocked, acknowledge the impact before moving to process.

## 5. Preserve trust during uncertainty

- Do not guess at causes, policy outcomes, or restoration timelines.
- If another team must review the case, explain the handoff clearly.
- If a previous response was incomplete or incorrect, correct it directly instead of writing around it.

## 6. Close with ownership

- End with the current owner or next review point.
- Make sure the response leaves no ambiguity about whether the thread is waiting on the customer or on us.

# Escalation Guidelines

Use this document to decide whether a support thread should stay in normal handling or move into a higher-touch workflow.

## Escalate immediately when

- there is a confirmed outage or broad degradation affecting multiple customers
- the customer cannot access billing, login, or account controls needed to operate normally
- there is a security, privacy, or account-compromise concern
- a customer reports potential data loss, corruption, or irreversible state changes
- a legal, compliance, or executive communications team needs to be involved

## Escalate after first response when

- the issue remains unresolved after the initial troubleshooting loop
- the customer is blocked on a deadline and needs a higher-confidence internal owner
- the issue crosses product, infrastructure, or billing boundaries and no single support responder can close it cleanly
- the thread has conflicting prior guidance that needs internal reconciliation before another public reply

## Keep in normal handling when

- the issue is a known workflow question with a documented answer
- the customer can continue working while waiting for a clarification
- the next action is a simple information request, reproduction step, or settings check

## Handoff expectations

When escalating:

- summarize the customer impact in one sentence
- state what has already been tried
- identify the exact owner or team you are handing off to
- note whether the customer is waiting on a fix, a decision, or a status update

## Billing and account-risk notes

- Do not promise credits, refunds, or restoration timelines unless policy or billing confirms them.
- If an account state looks unusual or risky, prefer escalation over speculative reassurance.
- If the customer may be locked out of a renewal, payment, or workspace-admin path, flag that as operationally urgent.
